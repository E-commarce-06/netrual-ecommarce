# Walmart: UX analysis

**Evidence basis:** DOM facts from `facts.json` (above-the-fold labels, placeholders, sticky state). Screenshots 01–12 are clean. Individual screenshots were not reviewed closely for this draft, so the visual claims are limited.

## 1. Reorder as a header action
- The desktop header shows "Reorder My Items" next to "Sign In Account" and a cart reading "$0.00".
- **Why:** a large share of grocery and household purchases repeat. A one-tap reorder path removes the search step for returning shoppers. The cart total shows the running cost, which reduces surprise at checkout.

## 2. Search that mentions where you can buy
- The search placeholder reads "Search everything at Walmart online and in store".
- **Why:** the placeholder sets the scope. Telling shoppers that results include in-store stock matches Walmart's omnichannel model.

## 3. Departments and Services as separate menus
- "Departments" and "Services" are top-level labelled buttons.
- **Why:** splitting shopping categories from services (pickup, pharmacy, financing) keeps the browse path separate from support tasks.

## 4. Hero carousel with pause control
- The home hero is a carousel with "Pause carousel" and next/previous controls.
- **Why:** carousels with visible controls respect motion sensitivity and let shoppers stop on a slide.

## 5. Mobile: menu, search, and an app banner
- The phone header has Menu, Search, and the cart, and a "Get the app" banner appears at the top.
- **Why:** the app banner trades a small amount of screen space for a route into app-only offers.

## Lessons for a re-skin
- Offer a reorder path if the store sells repeat items.
- Say what the search covers in the placeholder.

## What not to copy
- The first screen is crowded with promotions. Keep one primary message above the fold.
