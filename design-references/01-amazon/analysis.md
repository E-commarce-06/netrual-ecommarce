# Amazon: UX analysis

**Evidence basis:** screenshots 01–15 (viewed: home desktop 16:10, product phone 390). DOM facts from `facts.json`. Psychology notes are interpretation, not Amazon's stated intent.

## 1. Search is the first control, at the top of every page
- The search box sits in the header at full width on desktop, with the category selector ("All") attached to its left. On mobile it moves to a second row under the logo and the search stays visible.
- **Why here:** most Amazon sessions start with a known item. Placing search at the top-left reading start (F-pattern) makes it the fastest route. The scope selector shows that the search can be narrowed before typing, which lowers the cost of a mistake.

## 2. Delivery location sits next to search
- "Delivering to The Dalles 97058 · Update location" is in the header on desktop, and on mobile it sits under the search bar.
- **Why:** availability and delivery date decide whether a shopper buys. Putting the location in the header answers "can it reach me?" before the shopper reaches the product page.

## 3. Home page as a dense merchandising wall
- The hero is a carousel of seasonal tiles (Halloween, fall wardrobe, Apple Watch). Below it are rows of cards ("Shop Tory Burch", "Shop 450+ gift card brands") with a labelled arrow.
- **Why:** many entry points increase the chance of matching one shopper's intent. Each tile has a short label, which makes it easy to scan. The carousel has visible pause/play controls, which is an accessibility feature and also signals that motion is optional.

## 4. Mobile: category drawer and Prime as the main persuasion
- The home page shows "Join Prime" twice near the top and the category menu as the first control.
- **Why:** a free-shipping membership reduces the perceived cost of each order. Repeating the CTA on mobile is a deliberate trade-off between clutter and conversion.

## 5. Product page: buy box and variant choice above the fold
- On phone, the product page shows the title, rating (4.3 with a count), price context ("3K+ bought in past month"), and a large image gallery. Colour variants start under the gallery.
- **Why:** social proof (a rating with a review count, and "bought in past month") sits next to the title, before the shopper has to scroll. The gallery dots show that there are more images.

## Lessons for a re-skin
- Keep search and location at the top. Use scope selectors only if the catalogue is large.
- Show social proof next to the title, not in a tab.
- Repeat one primary membership CTA at most, not several.

## What not to copy
- The home page is dense and hard to scan for a new visitor. Use it only as a reference for merchandising density, not for hierarchy.
