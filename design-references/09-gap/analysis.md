# Gap: UX analysis

**Evidence basis:** DOM facts from `facts.json`. Screenshots are clean but were not reviewed closely for this draft. No product page was captured on this run.

## 1. Gender-first navigation
- The top navigation lists "Women", "Men", "Girls", "Boys", and "Baby & Toddler".
- **Why:** apparel shoppers usually know who the clothes are for. Splitting the catalogue by audience reduces the number of products shown at each step.

## 2. Bag count in the header
- The bag button is labelled "0 items in your shopping bag".
- **Why:** a visible count shows progress and keeps the cart in view. The label gives screen readers the same information.

## 3. Mobile: "Shop now" and a search button
- The phone home page has a "Shop now" call to action and an "Open search bar" control.
- **Why:** one primary CTA on a promotional screen gives a clear next step.

## Lessons for a re-skin
- Use audience or category as the first level of navigation for apparel.
- Keep one primary CTA on the first mobile screen.

## What not to copy
- Without a product page capture, product-detail patterns cannot be judged from this site.
