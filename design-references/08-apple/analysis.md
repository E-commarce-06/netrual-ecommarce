# Apple: UX analysis

**Evidence basis:** screenshots 01–15 (viewed: home desktop 16:10). DOM facts from `facts.json`.

## 1. Global navigation as a single dark bar
- The desktop header is a thin dark bar with the Apple logo, product categories ("Store", "Mac", "iPad", "iPhone", ...), then search and bag icons on the right.
- **Why:** the categories are the shopper's first decision. Keeping them on one line makes the choice visible at once. The dark bar recedes behind the hero.

## 2. Hero with a headline and two buttons of different weight
- The hero is black with a large product name ("iPhone 18 Pro"), a short line ("Pro further."), and two buttons: a filled blue "Learn more" and an outlined "Buy".
- **Why:** the filled button is the default action, and the outline button is the commitment action. Two levels of emphasis let the shopper choose between exploring and buying without a competing choice.

## 3. Product as a large, slow reveal
- The product image takes the centre of the hero. Below the fold, the next section is a light grey background with a new product name and a short tagline.
- **Why:** a single large product image uses scale to signal importance. The light section after the black hero creates a clear change of pace.

## 4. Search and bag are icons
- Search and bag are icon-only. The desktop bag icon has no count shown in this view.
- **Why:** Apple's store is small enough that search is secondary. The trade-off is that a first-time shopper needs to recognise the icon.

## 5. Mobile: "Buy" and "View pricing" as the main actions
- The phone view shows "Learn more", "Buy", and "View pricing" on product tiles.
- **Why:** pricing and buying are separated from information. "View pricing" is a lower-commitment action than "Buy", which suits a high-price purchase.

## Lessons for a re-skin
- Use two button weights in a hero: one filled, one outlined.
- Let the product image do the persuading in a high-price category.
- Put categories in a single horizontal bar on desktop.

## What not to copy
- Icon-only search and bag are hard to find for shoppers who don't already know them. Show the count and a label on desktop.
